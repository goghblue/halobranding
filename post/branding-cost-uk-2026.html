How much does branding cost in the UK? A 2026 price guide for small businesses

By Gina Ha, Founder & Brand Strategist, HALO Branding · Kingston upon Thames · Updated 6 November 2026

The short answer: in 2026, UK small businesses typically pay £500–£2,000 for a logo, £1,500–£5,000 for a logo with brand guidelines, £900–£2,500 for a brand audit and £2,000–£15,000 for brand strategy. A full rebrand from an agency often runs £5,000–£20,000 or more. The biggest price drivers are who does the work, whether strategy is included, and whether VAT is added on top.

Most UK branding agencies don't publish prices, so it's hard to know whether a quote is fair. We reviewed published UK pricing guides and agency price pages in November 2026 and put the typical ranges in one place. We've also listed HALO's own prices, because we publish ours, so you can compare.

Why branding prices vary so much

Who does the work. Freelancers usually charge £20–£75 an hour, and London rates can reach £150 an hour or more. Small studios price by project. Larger agencies add account management and overheads, which is why the same deliverable can cost three times as much.

Whether strategy is included. A logo-only project is cheap because nobody is asking what the business should stand for. Once research, positioning and messaging are included, the price moves into the thousands.

What "brand guidelines" means. One studio's guidelines are a two-page logo sheet; another's are a 40-page system. Ask for an example before comparing quotes.

VAT. VAT-registered agencies add 20%, and many published prices exclude it. A £5,000 quote can become £6,000.

What each service costs, and when you need it

Brand audit: £900–£2,500

A brand audit checks whether your website, brochure and other touchpoints say the same thing, and whether that is what customers need to hear. It's the right first step when you feel something is off but can't say what. Automated tools sit at the bottom of the market; agency audits for sectors such as law or accountancy can cost far more.

Brand strategy and positioning: £2,000–£15,000

Strategy defines what your business can genuinely claim that competitors can't, and how to say it. You need it before a rebrand, when you're entering a new market, or when customers can't tell you apart from competitors. Fixed-fee packages from smaller consultancies sit at the lower end; workshop-led agency programmes for mid-sized firms often start around £3,500.

Tone of voice and messaging (verbal identity): £500–£2,000

This covers what you say about your business and how you say it: your one-line description, key messages and writing rules. It's often cheaper than visual work and has an immediate effect on your website, proposals and social media.

Logo design: £500–£2,000

Freelancers typically charge £500–£1,000 and small agencies £1,000–£2,000. Online logo makers are cheaper but rarely give you exclusive rights or files that work in print.

Visual identity and brand guidelines: £1,500–£5,000

A logo plus colours, fonts, image style and the rules for using them. This is what keeps your brand looking like one business across your website, social media and print.

AI search visibility audit: £300–£1,500

A newer service. More customers now ask ChatGPT or Google's AI for recommendations before they visit any website. An audit checks what these tools say about your business and where they get it from. Free automated checkers exist; agency audits add interpretation and fixes.

Social media templates: £300–£800

Branded post templates, often in Canva, so your team can publish without a designer. Ongoing social media management is a separate monthly cost, typically £1,200 a month or more from agencies.

"My branding feels weak." Where should I start?

Most small businesses don't need everything at once. Start with the cheapest step that answers your real question:

  1. You're not sure what's wrong: start with a brand audit. It tells you what to fix before you spend on design.
  2. You sound like everyone else: you need positioning before new visuals, or the new logo will have nothing to say.
  3. Your position is clear but your materials look inconsistent: go straight to visual identity and guidelines.
  4. Your team describes the business differently: a tone of voice and messaging guide.
  5. Customers can't find you, or AI tools describe someone else: an AI search visibility audit and your Google Business Profile come first.

How to choose a branding agency: 6 questions to ask

  1. Can you give me a fixed price in writing? A clear scope and price protects you from surprises.
  2. Who will actually do the work? The person who pitches isn't always the person who delivers.
  3. Do you start by looking at evidence? Good branding starts from what your business really does well and what customers value, not from a mood board.
  4. What exactly is in the deliverables? Ask to see an example of the guidelines or report.
  5. Is VAT included?
  6. Will this help me show up in Google and AI search? Branding that doesn't help customers find you is only half the job in 2026.

Frequently asked questions

Is £500 enough for branding?

£500 usually buys a logo from a freelancer or a focused review of your current brand, not a full identity with strategy and guidelines.

Do I need brand strategy before a logo?

If you're unsure what makes your business different, yes. A logo designed without a clear position tends to be judged on taste alone. If your position is already clear, a designer can work from a short brief.

How long does branding take?

A brand audit usually takes one to two weeks, a logo two to three weeks, a visual identity with guidelines three to six weeks, and a full strategy-led rebrand two to four months.

Do UK branding agencies charge VAT?

VAT-registered agencies add VAT at 20%, and many published prices exclude it. Always ask whether a quote includes VAT.

Can I just use an AI logo generator or Canva?

For testing an idea, yes. For a business you plan to grow, you'll usually want a logo you own exclusively, files that work in print, and a look built on what your business stands for.
